<div class="page-heading">
  <h1>SACCO &amp; MFB Due Diligence</h1>
</div>

<section class="section--tight">
  <p class="methodology-statement">A bank depositor has decades of analyst coverage, ratings agencies, and financial press watching the institution holding their money. A SACCO or MFB member, investor, or lender has almost none of that. No equivalent independent coverage exists at the individual-institution level. This report builds it.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">Who this is for</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">For anyone about to extend credit, commit capital, or place trust in a specific SACCO or microfinance bank without an independent, evidence-based read on its current condition, whether that is an institutional lender or investor, or a member or oversight committee seeking a clearer picture of their own institution.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">Problems this addresses</h2>
  <ul class="tier__list" style="max-width:60ch;">
    <li>Members and smaller investors have no equivalent of bank equity research or ratings coverage for individual SACCOs. A real, currently unaddressed information gap, with real Kenyan precedent for what happens when it goes wrong.</li>
    <li>SASRA supervision is generally perceived as less rigorous and less timely than CBK oversight of banks. Independent verification carries more weight where regulatory oversight cannot be fully relied on.</li>
    <li>Total whitespace on institutional coverage: no major data or ratings provider covers SACCOs or MFBs individually.</li>
    <li>Governance risk specific to the cooperative structure: member-elected boards, often without deep financial oversight capacity, create a known pattern of related-party or insider lending to board members and officials.</li>
    <li>Scope creep beyond the original mandate: many SACCOs have expanded into FOSA (front office service activity) products functioning like retail banking, layering on risk the membership may not fully understand.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">What you get</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">An evidence-based read of the institution's current condition, adapted to how SACCOs and MFBs are actually structured and regulated:</p>
  <ul class="tier__list" style="max-width:60ch;">
    <li>Financial condition: asset quality, funding structure, and capital position, drawn from SASRA filings and published statements</li>
    <li>Governance structure: board composition and election process, oversight capacity, and related-party lending exposure specific to the cooperative model</li>
    <li>Regulatory and compliance record: drawn from SASRA filings, Gazette notices, and court records where they exist</li>
    <li>Scope of activity: including FOSA and other product lines beyond the core savings and credit mandate</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Every finding is traceable to a named, dated public source. Same discipline as every other report on this site.</p>
</section>

<div class="policy-line">
  Depth of evidence varies by institution and by what is actually disclosed under the SASRA regime, generally thinner and less standardized than commercial bank disclosure under CBK. Where the public record is limited for a given institution, that is stated plainly, not padded to look more complete than it is.
</div>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">What it is, and isn't</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Not a credit rating. Not a recommendation to join, lend to, or invest in the institution. Not a guarantee of financial soundness. An independent, sourced account of what the public record currently shows.</p>
</section>
