<div class="page-heading">
  <h1>Cross-Border Group Exposure Read</h1>
</div>

<section class="section--tight">
  <p class="methodology-statement">No single-country regulator, and no individual subsidiary's own reporting, can see what is building up across a banking group's footprint. Each is watching only its own jurisdiction. This report is built for the Kenya piece of that view, read in the context of the group around it.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">Who this is for</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">For anyone holding exposure to a banking group's Kenyan subsidiary who needs to understand it in the context of the wider group: a parent-level risk function, a regional DFI, or a shareholder with cross-border exposure.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">Problems this addresses</h2>
  <ul class="tier__list" style="max-width:60ch;">
    <li>No single-country regulator or subsidiary self-reporting provides an independent cross-check on group-wide correlated risk.</li>
    <li>A live cross-border event, a regulatory approval, a ruling, a deal, can create risk faster than internal group-level aggregation responds to it.</li>
    <li>A DFI or shareholder with exposure across several subsidiaries in one group otherwise has to assemble several disconnected national reports themselves, with no single comparative read doing that work for them.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">What you get</h2>
  <ul class="tier__list" style="max-width:60ch;">
    <li>A full evidence-based read of the group's Kenyan subsidiary: the same six-dimension standard and the same sourcing discipline as Counterparty Intelligence Reports</li>
    <li>A cross-country macro and fiscal comparison: FX regime differences, capital controls, and sovereign risk differentials across the group's footprint, used as the structural lens that puts the Kenya findings in group context</li>
    <li>Correlation and concentration risk flagged where the Kenya evidence and the macro and fiscal comparison point to it, structured around what no single-country view can see</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<div class="policy-line">
  This is not full, court-level evidence-sourcing depth across every country in the group's footprint. Sourcing rigor at that depth is currently built for Kenya: primary-source court records, registry filings, and gazette notices. The cross-country element is a macro and fiscal comparison, not equivalent on-the-ground evidence work in every jurisdiction. Where a claim depends on evidence outside Kenya, that is stated plainly rather than implied.
</div>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">What it is, and isn't</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Not a consolidated group rating. Not a substitute for the group's own internal risk aggregation. Not a prediction of contagion. It states where correlated exposure is evidenced, not what will happen as a result.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">How it works</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Scoped after an initial conversation about the group's footprint and which jurisdictions and subsidiaries are relevant. Delivered as a structured written report; turnaround agreed upfront based on scope.</p>
</section>
