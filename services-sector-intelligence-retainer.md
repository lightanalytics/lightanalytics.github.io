<div class="page-heading">
  <h1>Sector Intelligence Retainer</h1>
</div>

<section class="section--tight">
  <p class="methodology-statement">A rising NPL figure means something different depending on whether it is specific to one institution or moving across the sector with it. Anyone can report that a number changed. This retainer's job is telling you which kind of change you are looking at, and whether your own review cycle would have caught it in time.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">Who this is for</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">For institutions that need to know if something has changed since their last review: not just what a counterparty looks like today, but whether it is still what it was when the relationship, credit line, or investment was first approved.</p>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Coverage is not restricted to a listing status or tier. It is scoped per client to the institutions relevant to their actual exposure.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">What you get: two formats</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">The underlying monitoring runs continuously against public-record evidence: court judgments, CBK enforcement notices, Gazette filings, governance and ownership changes. That feed is delivered in whichever format fits how it is actually used.</p>
  <ul class="tier__list" style="max-width:60ch;">
    <li>Watchlist maintenance: a defined, documented list of counterparties under close monitoring, with the rationale for every addition and removal recorded. Built to stand up to internal audit or regulator questions about how counterparty risk is monitored between scheduled reviews.</li>
    <li>Board-ready briefings: a short, distilled risk narrative delivered ahead of your board or risk committee's own meeting cadence, not a data dump. Built to be read in minutes, not analyzed for hours.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Each flagged change is classified as institution-specific or reflective of a sector-wide or macro move, the distinction that determines whether it actually matters, with an explicit flag on anything a normal review cycle would likely have missed.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">What it is, and isn't</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Not a forecast. Not a prediction of failure. Not a trigger for automatic action. A flagged change is a prompt to look closer, not a conclusion. The decision on what to do with it stays with the buyer.</p>
</section>

<section class="section--tight">
  <h2 class="section-title">How it works</h2>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Cadence, coverage, and format are scoped per engagement, agreed upfront based on what is relevant to the client's exposure and how their own review and board cycles run.</p>
  <p class="methodology-statement">Retainer capacity is limited by design. Quality and attentiveness on a recurring product matter more than volume, so slots are capped rather than open-ended.</p>
</section>
